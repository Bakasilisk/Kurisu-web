Only the ranked leaderboards fill their panel (.lb-table sets width: 100%). The "this is you" row takes --row-highlight at 650 weight with a 4px inset brand rail on the first cell — a token kept independent of the heatmap ramp, so the leaderboard isn't coupled to it. The highlight is a wash that fades out across the row, and it sits on the <tr>, not the cells: per-cell the gradient restarts in every <td> and visibly bands across the row. Rows hover to --brand-softer rather than a flat grey swap.

Column headers — rows with no <td> in them, matched via tr:not(:has(td)) — take a faint band so the header separates from the body. That selector is what distinguishes a real header row from the row-label <th> the key/value tables use; engines without :has() simply skip the band.

Rank medals

1 2 3

The top three ranks used to be 🥇🥈🥉. Emoji rendered at a different width than the digits below them, so the # column lost its alignment, and each platform drew a different medal. .rank-medal is a 1.7em circle holding the real numeral, sized in em so it tracks the table's font size rather than pinning to a pixel value.

The three gradients are the one place in the stylesheet with hard-coded hex outside the token block, and deliberately so: gold, silver and bronze are literal material references, not brand colors, and they carry the same meaning in both schemes. White text on all three clears AA at the weight they're set in. Rank 4 and below stay bare tabular numerals — medaling everything would flatten the distinction the component exists to make.

Key/value tables stay at their natural width — stretched to the full 1100px column, a label and its value drift to opposite edges and stop reading as a pair. Row-header cells drop the uppercase tracking via tr:has(td) > th, since that treatment shouts on a long label like "Reactions given/received". Engines without :has() keep the uppercase form.
