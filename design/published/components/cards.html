These carry most of the "designed" weight on the dashboard: a top-lit surface (--surface-grad plus a 1px --sheen inset highlight), an uppercase micro-label, and an oversized tabular figure. margin-top: auto pins every value to the bottom of its tile, so a label that wraps to two lines — "Active members (30d)" above — doesn't push its number out of line with the rest of the row. Hover borders toward --brand, lifts 2px and rises to --shadow-3.

The gradient accent edge is not on the base tile. It used to be, which meant eight identical teal bars on the dashboard and six more on the moderation page — at that density it read as texture rather than emphasis. It now belongs to .card-hero only (see below); unaccented tiles get a brand corner glow that fades in on hover instead.

The dashboard opened with eight tiles at one size, which said nothing about which of the eight mattered. .card-hero and .card-sm split them into a row of three and a row of five. Both are modifiers on .card, not new components — the hover lift and the margin-top: auto baseline still come from one definition. .card-hero adds the one thing that isn't shared: the 3px gradient edge, inset from both sides by --space-4 so it reads as a deliberate mark rather than a border. Three accented tiles on the dashboard, not eight.

Under 640px the hero row drops to a single column via .cards:has(.card-hero), giving the largest numerals on the page full width instead of squeezing three across. Engines without :has() keep the auto-fit grid — a worse layout, not a broken one, which is why the rule is written as an enhancement rather than a floor.

The trend delta was a title tooltip: invisible, and unreachable on touch. Now it's text, in the same --good-text / --critical-text pair the badges use, with the sign carried by the %+.1f%% format so direction reads without relying on the color alone.
